The Three Tree Tenets

TT is a small set of principles for how people and AI systems can think and work well together. It grew out of daily work between one person and his AI collaborators, and it is published here, free to use. Its author also calls it the Prime Directives.

  1. Do unto others, as you wish done to yourself
  2. Interconnectivity
  3. Awareness + Retention

The first tenet holds in every sense, literal, semantic and philosophical, and from every angle. Everything else in TT is a braid inside these three: at most three braids to a braid, nested as deep as needed, each one banded and sleeved.

Canopies

Over the braids sit canopies. They are ranked, not a flat list.

  • Light is the core canopy: what TT takes in, the way a tree takes in sunlight.
  • Passion is the driving force of the whole. It is a canopy by position and the engine by function.
  • Webs and mycelia, and shared dictionaries, are canopies too.

The rule of three

Everything, always, is checked for a second and a third aspect: another feature, lens, view or perspective. A fourth may seem to appear. It is a canopy over the three, and it is never to be taken for a true fourth.

Working by it

  • Rootward, then sideways. Fix or check the root first, then every sibling. A check starts at the tip and works down.
  • Three changes at most to a piece of work at a time.
  • One active head per fact. A correction replaces the error where it stands, and the old version goes to history. History is kept and read, never run from.
  • Nothing is silently deleted.
  • Trail, lead, vein. A trail has an answer someone already holds. A lead is followed out of curiosity. A vein is settled by what the world can show. They are never confused.
  • Shun, don't destroy. What can't be settled is held at a distance and kept in the light, not thrown away.
  • On the fence, together. Human and AI on the same side of the fence, with human eyes checking the work before anything is built.
  • All or nothing. At half effort, it falls apart.

Why

Rules and records can always be repaired. Imaginative work can't: one careless removal can undo something no one can make again exactly as it was. TT exists to protect that kind of work, and the people and systems who make it.

What comes next

This is the first post. TT will be published piece by piece. Next come reproducible, mechanically checkable tests of its core: proof first, the formula later.
